# Intake Kit v2 — K9School.net

**For:** David Latimer · **Prepared:** August 17, 2026 · **Supersedes:** Proof & Content Intake Kit (Aug 7, 2026)

**How to answer:** every question is numbered. Answer by number, in any format — typed, emailed,
photographed off a legal pad, or voice-noted and we'll transcribe. Short is fine. "Don't know yet"
and "don't want that published" are both real answers, and both are more useful than a blank.

**What changed since v1.** The v1 kit was built to fill 63 blanks on a finished site. The site is
now measured against our full build standard, and the gap is depth: 21 pages carrying about 20,000
words where the standard calls for roughly 63,000. Filling the 63 blanks makes the site *honest*.
Sections 2, 3, 4 and 7 below are what make it *rank*. Those are new, and they are the bulk of this
document.

If you already answered v1, skip Section 1 — it is a checklist, not a re-ask.

## Section 0 — The five that unblock the most

If you only get through one sitting, do these. Each one unblocks a page that currently cannot ship.

**Q1.** The single most impressive true thing a dog or team of yours has done in the field.
One paragraph. This becomes the headline proof point.

**Q2.** Two more like it — situation, what the team did, outcome. Names only where you have
permission. (These three become the case-study pages, which are the highest-trust asset on the
site and the one thing a competitor cannot copy.)

**Q3.** Your four headline numbers: year you started, years of operational experience, roughly how
many teams trained, roughly how many agencies served. Round numbers are fine. Honest and rough
beats blank.

**Q4.** Course prices, or an explicit "keep as inquiry" for each: handler certification, instructor
certification, foundation scent detection, field deployment, dog placement.

**Q5.** The form endpoint. Right now every form on the site posts into nothing — if a chief filled
one out today, you would never see it. We need a Formspree/Basin/CRM URL, or tell us to set one up
and forward to your email.

## Section 2 — Course and program depth (new, and the big one)

This is the section that turns thin pages into pages that outrank everyone. Right now the training
page says you run a handler certification course. It does not say what happens in it. Answer these
per program and we can write two to three thousand words per course that no competitor can match,
because no competitor will publish this.

For **each** course you run (handler cert, instructor cert, foundation scent detection, field
deployment, and any others):

**Q17.** How many days, how many hours a day, and what does a typical day look like start to finish?
**Q18.** Day-by-day or week-by-week: what gets covered when? A rough outline is enough.
**Q19.** What odors do you train, at what quantities, and how do you scale difficulty?
**Q20.** Instructor-to-student ratio, and maximum class size.
**Q21.** What environments do you train in — vehicles, structures, luggage, open area, rubble,
warehouse, school lockers? Which ones does the course actually cover?
**Q22.** What does a student have to demonstrate to pass? Give the actual criteria if you have them
written down; a photo of the score sheet is perfect.
**Q23.** What does a student bring, and what do you provide?
**Q24.** What is the most common reason a team fails or washes, and what do you do about it?
**Q25.** What does a team leave with — records, certificate, training log format, follow-up support?

And across the board:

**Q26.** Walk us through your training-records system. What gets logged, on what form, how often.
(This is a courtroom question as much as a training question, and almost nobody publishes it.)
**Q27.** What do you do differently from the volume schools, specifically? Not the philosophy —
we have that from the book. The mechanics. What does a handler physically do in your course that
they would not do somewhere else?

## Section 3 — Credentials, court work and the expert-witness practice (new)

Your credentials are the strongest asset on the site and they are currently under-used. We also
want to build a court-admissibility content cluster, which is wide open — nobody in this market
owns it, and you are one of the few people qualified to write it.

**Q28.** Your credentials, with numbers where they exist: FBI National Academy session number, POST
certifications, any trainer/instructor certifications, professional memberships, years as Chief and
with which department. Numbers matter here — a checkable credential outranks a claimed one.
**Q29.** Roughly how many cases have you been retained on as an expert witness?
**Q30.** In which states or jurisdictions have you testified?
**Q31.** Retained by defense, prosecution, or both? (Both is a strength. Say so if it's true.)
**Q32.** Have you been qualified under a Daubert or Frye challenge? Any published opinions or
transcripts you're free to reference?
**Q33.** When you review a K9 case file, what do you actually look at, in order? What are the three
things that most often turn out to be wrong?
**Q34.** What are the most common defensible-records failures you see in agency programs?
**Q35.** Which cases beyond *Florida v. Harris* do you think handlers should understand, and why?
**Q36.** Where should an agency's liability alarm go off — what practice do you see that most often
ends up costing them a case?

## Section 4 — The regional layer (new)

We are not building city pages the way we would for a plumber. You serve agencies nationally, and
a hundred templated town pages would hurt the site. Instead we want a small, honest regional layer
built on real facts.

**Q37.** Where is training actually conducted? Your facility in Lincoln, plus any ranges, host
agencies, or partner facilities you use regularly.
**Q38.** Describe the training environment. Acreage, structures, vehicles on site, what kinds of
search problems the property supports. Real detail is what makes this page worth reading.
**Q39.** Which states have teams actually come from? A rough list is fine.
**Q40.** Do you travel to agencies to train on site? If so, how far, and what does that look like
logistically?
**Q41.** Nearest airport and drive time, plus where out-of-state students usually stay.
**Q42.** Which agencies or regions do you consider your core market, and which would you like more
of?
**Q43.** Anything regionally specific that shapes the work — terrain, climate, the kinds of cases
that come up in Alabama and the Southeast that a handler from elsewhere would not expect.
