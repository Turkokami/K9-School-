## Section 3 — What they got right, and what we changed this week

Four things were genuinely missing. Three are now done.

**1. The method had no name.** This was the largest point in both reports and they
were correct. Competitors lead with a name a reader can repeat ten seconds later —
Hare's "On Target," Ford's "Odor Pays." The Method page now names the
**Latimer Detector Dog System** and the **Behavior-First Detection** approach, and
states the division of labour plainly: *the handler controls the search assignment;
odor controls the detection decision.*

That sentence is the sharpest thing in either report. ChatGPT is right that it is
stronger than saying the dog should work independently, because a dog that will not
take direction is not an operational dog. It is now on the site in those words.

**2. The research was referred to but never cited.** The site said "Dr. Lisa Lit's
well-known work." It now cites the study properly — Lit, Schweitzer and Oberbauer
(2011), *Animal Cognition* — with the actual finding: eighteen certified teams,
164 searches of rooms containing no target odor, 225 incorrect alerts clustering
where handlers believed odor was. The study is also marked up in the page code so
search engines and AI assistants can see the page is sourcing real research.

We also added the qualification both reports asked for: the size of the effect
depends on how strongly the handler believes, and some of it is the handler reading
ambiguity as an alert rather than cueing the dog into one. Both directions produce
the same problem for a court. Saying so makes the page harder to attack, not softer.

**3. The Five Phases were presented as established fact.** They were described as
"the behavioral sequence every detector-dog search moves through." ChatGPT flagged
this and it was the most useful catch in either document.

Research supports the pieces — that behavior before the final response carries
information, and that dogs measurably change how they move as they close on source.
What no published study has done is validate that every search divides into these
exact five phases, or that two observers would draw the boundaries the same way.

It is now **the Latimer Five-Phase Model**, described as a working framework built
in the field, consistent with the research but not a finding of it. The page says
so in plain language, and explains why that matters: a handler who testifies to
"the five scientifically established phases of canine detection" has handed the
other side an easy afternoon. A handler who says "I use a five-phase framework to
describe what I observed, and here is what the dog did" is describing evidence.

This is a smaller change than it sounds and a bigger protection than it sounds.

**4. Odor science was thin.** The Method page now has a section on the dog smelling
vapor rather than objects, and why that changes a search — why source can be feet
from where odor is strongest, why an inaccessible source produces a dog that will
not settle, why residual odor exists. That is the ground your Myers collaboration
sits on. See Q70 before we name him.

Detection™. We will not put a ™ on the site until you confirm the exact protected
wording and that a registration or claim actually exists. Publishing a trademark

**We did not name Dr. Myers yet.** Naming a real person as a collaborator is his
call as much as yours. The odor-science section is written and there is a flag on
the page where his name goes. See Q70.

**We did not adopt the hero copy Grok wrote.** It is decent, but it says "25 years"
where your site says trainer since 1999, which is 27, and the number on the site
came out of your book. One of the two is wrong. See Q74.

**We did not write anything about learned helplessness.** ChatGPT found you taught a
session on it in 2021 and called it the part of your theory most exposed to a
scientific challenge. It is not on the site and we would leave it that way, or use
"handler dependence," which describes the same behavior without the claim.

## Section 5 — Two decisions that are yours

**Decision 1 — three brands or one site?**

Both reports recommend the same architecture:

> Behavior Is Evidence explains why. LSOC teaches how. The K9 Alliance tests the result.

As three separate properties. We are currently building one site that does all
three jobs. That is a real fork and we are not going to pick it for you.

Our read: the three-brand split is right eventually and probably premature now. One
strong site that ranks beats three thin ones that do not, and everything we are
building can be lifted into a separate Behavior Is Evidence site later without being
rewritten. But if the book is close to publication, the argument for splitting sooner
gets stronger. Tell us which way you are leaning and we will build to it.

**Decision 2 — the K9 Alliance needs governance before it gets promoted.**

This is the most serious item in either report and we agree with it completely.

ChatGPT puts the problem as a question a defense attorney will eventually ask:
*Latimer created the training system, trained the teams, created the certifying
body, trained the evaluators, and then certified handlers using his own method?*

That question does not make the Alliance illegitimate. It makes it attackable, and
it lands hardest on the one brand whose entire argument is courtroom defensibility.
The answer is structural: written standards, published scoring criteria, evaluator
qualification requirements, conflict-of-interest rules, and at least some evaluators
who are not you. That is fixable, but it should be fixed before the Alliance is
promoted rather than after someone raises it in a hearing.

ChatGPT also reports the Alliance site currently has a prototype verification
database and a placeholder email still live. Worth checking before any traffic goes
there.
