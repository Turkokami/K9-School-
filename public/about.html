About

Operator. Trainer. Expert witness.

K9School is the working home of Latimer School of Operational K9s — David Latimer's operational detection practice in Lincoln, Alabama, built on a career spent in the field and in the courtroom.

David Latimer

A lifetime around working dogs.

David Latimer's work with dogs began long before his first detection deployment — it began as a boy, watching his father work a liver-spotted pointer named Sport with nothing but his voice and a quiet kind of respect. That was positive reinforcement before it had a name, and compound behavior before anyone called it that. The lesson stuck: the dog was always communicating; the job was to listen.

After the military — where he served as an HVAC technician and then a small-arms marksmanship instructor — a chance friendship with a forensic engineer pulled David into the world of fire and explosion investigation. He trained his first accelerant-detection dog in 1999, worked hundreds of fire scenes, and helped put arsonists in prison. From there came narcotics, explosives, cadaver, and tracking dogs, and pest-detection dogs beginning around 2002.

He attended the police academy in 2003 and served as a law enforcement officer for the rest of his career, promoted to Chief of Police in 2007 before retiring from full-time law enforcement in 2015. Along the way he graduated from the FBI National Academy in Quantico — the highlight of his LE career. Today he trains, certifies, consults, and testifies, and has written extensively on detector-dog behavior and reliability.

  • FBI National Academy graduate (Quantico, VA).
  • Retired Chief of Police; law enforcement officer since 2003.
  • Training detector dogs since 1999 — arson/accelerant, narcotics, explosives, cadaver, tracking, and pest detection.
  • Author and instructor on detector-dog behavior, reliability, and courtroom defensibility.

The dog behind the name

Kip.

One of the finest detector dogs David ever worked was an accelerant-detection dog named Kip — the namesake of the original Kip K9 brand. What set Kip apart wasn't drive or flash. It was honesty.

Kip worked because he wanted to solve the odor problem. If target odor was there, he found it. If it wasn't, he refused to manufacture an answer just because someone expected one. More than once, people questioned his decisions — and more than once, Kip was right and the assumptions were wrong. That honesty became one of David's greatest teachers, and it's the standard every dog here is built toward.

"Kip worked because he wanted to solve the odor problem. He did not seem compelled to manufacture an answer simply because someone expected one."— David Latimer
